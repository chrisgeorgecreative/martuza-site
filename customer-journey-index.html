Case study 02 · Customer experience

The Customer Journey Index

Mapping planned digital support across common customer experiences.

Role
Service designer, lead
Timeline
2023 — 4 months
Focus
Digital channel strategy
Hero image — 16:9

The challenge

The organization was structured by channel, with each channel team pursuing its own goals independently — often solving the same customer problem more than once, from scratch, on parallel tracks.

Because there was no shared view across channels, teams routinely re-ran the same discovery work to solve problems other teams had already tackled elsewhere. The result showed up for customers too: experiences that didn’t line up with their actual channel preferences, and journeys that broke down the moment someone switched from one channel to another mid-task. We had a clear mandate to look across every channel — but this was a visioning exercise, not an implementation project, and it still meant earning the trust of the business owners across all four operating companies before any of it could move.

The approach

I built the Customer Journey Index — a rolled-up view of how well each channel actually supported the journeys customers were living, validated directly with the people who owned each channel.

We started from existing data, using AI-assisted analysis to fill initial gaps, and landed on roughly eleven core customer experiences to anchor the work. From there, we ran workshops with the relevant business owners and their SMEs — first across the customer journey as a whole, then a dedicated session per experience — to map current-state reality, known pain points, and a shared vision for how each experience could work three to five years out. Each experience was then detailed against every channel meant to support it, step by step, and rolled up into the Index itself so gaps and duplication across channels became visible at a glance. Keeping the workshops smaller and more focused than the norm at the time turned out to matter as much as the framework — once business owners saw their own thinking reflected back and taken seriously, engagement followed.

The outcome

The Index gave the group its first shared language for talking about these experiences — the honest next challenge is turning dense artifacts into something a fast-moving organisation will actually keep using.

The clearest win was adoption of a common taxonomy: the organisation now uses these customer experiences as the shared hook for how it talks about the journey at all, which is a real first step toward alignment across four previously siloed operating companies. It was well received — business owners saw the value immediately — and it’s already informing active reinvention work on the web channel. Progress elsewhere has been slower, partly because the artifacts are dense for a busy workplace to engage with day-to-day, and partly because organisational focus has shifted toward platform alignment amid wider structural change. The clear next step is an engagement plan that teaches teams to use these maps as intended, rather than treating them as a one-time reference.
